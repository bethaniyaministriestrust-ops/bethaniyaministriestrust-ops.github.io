A story of local presence

Our history

Roots in Walajabad

From worship and education to medical care.

Bethany World Mission grew from a vision to reach the villages around Walajabad through medical care and education. Walajabad sits between Kanchipuram and Chengalpattu, near Chennai, surrounded by villages and agricultural lands.

By 1995, Rev. David Kennedy began developing Bethany Mission. After arriving in the United States, he helped register the mission and form a board of dedicated servants.

1995

Rev. David Kennedy began developing Bethany Mission's vision for village service.

2003

Land was purchased and a church building was raised in Palavery village.

2004

Bethany's registered ministry continued growing through health, education, and community programs.

Our programs

Programs in practice

Care, learning, shelter, and community built around local needs.

  1. Capacity Building and Skills Development Program

    Practical computer education and career preparation support young people facing barriers to further study and work. Training includes spoken English, English typewriting, accountancy, computer applications, and personal development.

  2. Health Care

    Community health care responds to poverty and barriers to medical services, with attention to primary care, appropriate technology, disease prevention, and maternal health.

  3. Rag Pickers

    Waste pickers play an important role in recycling. The program supports self-awareness, shelter, recreation, and stronger connections with the wider community.

  4. LGBT & CSW

    People in LGBT communities and commercial sex workers deserve dignity, inclusion, health care, skills development, spiritual care, and family support.

  5. Church Planting

    Training and conferences equip pastors and local leaders, support congregations with few resources, and encourage sustained church growth.

  6. Home for the Homeless

    A shared home can bring older adults, people in poverty, and children under one roof, where residents can support one another. The plan includes housing, food, clothing, education, and health care.

Strengthening care in remote communities

Our current needs

Childcare and nutrition

Need for a Community Hall with Kitchen and Childcare Facilities

Bethany works among underprivileged communities living in remote hamlets, where families face significant economic and social challenges. Most parents depend on daily-wage labour, food gathering, and fishing in nearby rivers for their livelihood. Some are engaged in other forms of manual labour for meagre wages. Unfortunately, there is no guarantee of regular employment, and securing sufficient work throughout the week remains a major challenge for these families.

Recognising their difficult circumstances, Bethany has taken initiatives to support these vulnerable families by distributing rice and providing milk powder for young children. In addition, Bethany provides food to families in the hamlets on a weekly basis to help address their nutritional needs.

However, there is an urgent need for a permanent community facility to expand and strengthen these services. Therefore, Bethany proposes to construct a Community Hall with a Kitchen. This facility will serve as a centre for operating day-care services for young children, conducting non-formal education programmes, and providing regular nutritional support.

The proposed Community Hall will offer children a safe and supportive environment where they can receive nutritious food, basic care, educational support, and opportunities for their overall development. It will also serve as a common space for community activities and programmes aimed at improving the well-being of underprivileged families.

Through this initiative, Bethany hopes to address the immediate nutritional and educational needs of children while contributing to the long-term development and empowerment of vulnerable communities.

Emergency medical transport

Need for a Medical Ambulance for Emergency Response

Bethany operates in remote areas where access to public transportation and hospital facilities is limited. Bethany provides first aid and basic medical assistance to people affected by medical emergencies. However, when patients require further medical attention, they must be transported to District Headquarters Hospitals, which are approximately 40 minutes to one hour away.

In addition to the travel time, patients often have to wait for an ambulance to become available, resulting in critical delays in receiving medical treatment. During medical emergencies, every minute is crucial in saving a person's life. Timely transportation and access to appropriate medical care can make a significant difference in patient survival.

Therefore, Bethany urgently requires a properly equipped medical ambulance to strengthen its emergency response services, ensure timely transportation of patients, and provide essential medical support while transferring them to hospitals. This facility will help Bethany respond more effectively to medical emergencies and serve vulnerable communities in remote areas.
